Our Programs

Structured housing, paid work, and help with the stuff in between

Five pieces, one plan. This is what living and working with Reborn Ready actually looks like, the good parts and the strict parts.

The five pillars

What you get, and what we expect

Every resident works through all five. They overlap on purpose. A job means nothing if you can't get to it, and a bed means nothing if you can't keep it.

1

Structured Housing

A real house, with real rules

Shared, rule-based homes on regular streets. We recommend a three to six month stay. That's enough time to get steady, save some money, and fix your paperwork. Not so long that you get comfortable and stop moving.

  • Furnished shared bedrooms and common areas
  • House rules, curfew, and chore rotation
  • Drug- and alcohol-free, no exceptions
2

Accountability & Skill Building

Check-ins and weekly workshops

Everybody does individual check-ins. Not to catch you slipping, to catch problems early. Weekly group workshops cover the stuff nobody teaches you inside:

  • Budgeting and actually saving a paycheck
  • Job searching, applications, and interviews
  • Conflict resolution, because shared houses get tense
3

Transportation Support

Getting where you have to be

Macon isn't an easy town without a car. Missing one probation meeting can undo months of work. We set up rides through local transit and ride-share partners so residents make it to:

  • Job interviews and work shifts
  • Medical and counseling appointments
  • Court dates and supervision check-ins
4

Resource Navigation

Case management without the runaround

We help you work through the boring, brutal paperwork of coming home, and we connect you to the people who can help with what we can't.

  • Getting your ID, Social Security card, and birth certificate back
  • Benefits enrollment and court obligations
  • Referrals to job placement, mental health counseling, and addiction recovery services
5

Community Integration

People who know your name

Isolation sends people back faster than almost anything. Regular community activities, service days, and family-friendly events help residents build a circle that isn't the old one. This is also where our veterans track lives.

Workforce training

Earn while you learn

This is the part I'm proudest of. Residents can train with GaChi Handyman Services, a working handyman company here in Middle Georgia. Repairs, painting, porches, drywall, the everyday stuff homeowners and landlords pay for.

You're not watching videos in a classroom. You're on a job site, with a crew, getting paid. By the time you leave, you've got skills, money saved, and somebody who can vouch for you on a job application. That last part matters more than people think.

Not everybody wants to swing a hammer, and that's fine. We'll also help with job searches, applications, and connections to other local employers.

Specialized tracks

For folks with extra weight to carry

Open now

Veterans Track

For justice-involved veterans. Housing priority, help navigating VA benefits (which, let's be honest, is a full-time job by itself), peer support, and connections to veteran-friendly employers.

You served. Then you got jammed up. That doesn't erase the first part.

Planned for Year 2

Recovery Support Track

For adults in recovery from substance use. Stable housing as the foundation for staying sober, plus peer recovery coaching and connections to treatment. We'd rather build this right than build it fast.

A normal week

What the schedule looks like

Every house runs a little different and every resident's schedule bends around work. But here's the general shape of a week.

WhenWhat happens
WeekdaysWork shifts, training on GaChi job sites, or job search hours. Up and out in the morning, not sleeping till noon.
Once a weekOne-on-one check-in. Goals, money, problems, what's next.
Once a weekGroup workshop: budgeting, job search, conflict resolution, and rotating life-skills topics.
As neededRides to probation or parole, court, medical, counseling, and interviews.
Every dayCurfew, house chores, and keeping your space clean. Sounds small. It isn't.
MonthlyCommunity activity or service day with the house.

Who we serve

Who can apply

Adults 18 and up who want the structure and are ready to work for it. That's the main thing. Here's who we're set up for:

  • Adults coming home from state prison or county jail, especially folks on parole or probation
  • Justice-involved veterans
  • Adults leaving recovery programs or homelessness who need a structured next step
  • People willing to follow house rules, stay drug- and alcohol-free, and work or train
  • Folks with ties to Macon-Bibb County and Middle Georgia

Being straight with you: right now we can't accept anyone with violent charges on their record. That's not a judgment on anybody. It's what we can handle safely in a shared house with the staff we have today. If that changes, we'll update this page.

How a referral works

  1. You, a family member, a case manager, or a probation or parole officer calls or emails us.
  2. We do a short phone screening. Release date, supervision, what you need.
  3. We meet, in person or on the phone, and go over the house rules together.
  4. When a bed opens up, you move in. Bring your paperwork, whatever you have. We'll help with the rest.
