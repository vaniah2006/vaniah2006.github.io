Why we're here

Getting out is one day. Staying out is every day after.

Here's what nobody says out loud. A lot of people walk out of a Georgia prison with a bag of paperwork, a release date, and maybe a phone number for a cousin. No lease. No ID half the time. No ride to the probation office. Then everybody acts surprised when they end up back inside.

Georgia has about 2,344 transitional center beds for somewhere between 14,000 and 16,000 people released every year. That's room for fewer than 15 out of every 100. Everybody else is on their own the day they walk out.

That's the gap. That's where we work. Not with a brochure. With a room, a set of house rules, a paycheck, and people who actually answer the phone.

How it works

Three steps. No fluff.

The whole program fits on an index card. Doing it is the hard part, and that's why we stay with you the whole way.

Move into a structured house

A shared home in Middle Georgia with daily accountability and clear house expectations. Long enough to get your feet under you, short enough that it doesn't turn into another holding pattern.

Get work-ready

Employment readiness, life-skills coaching, and reentry guidance. Resumes, interviews, IDs, referrals, whatever's standing between you and a paycheck. We track your goals so you can see yourself move.

Leave with a plan that holds

Savings habits, a budget you've actually lived on, references, your documents back in hand, and a place lined up. Then you go. And we're still a phone call away.

A separate company that secures, leases, and manages the houses our residents live in. Keeping it separate means the nonprofit can focus on people while the LLC handles the property side.

A working handyman business in Middle Georgia and a partner for hands-on work experience. When there's a fit, residents can pick up real job-site skills and a reference.

Who's behind this

Hey, I'm Chrissy.

Founder & Director, Reborn Ready Corp

I've spent years around young people who got caught up early. Seven years running a youth-at-risk group. Six years running a program for underage first-time offenders. Plus years doing admin and program work inside a police department.

I kept seeing the same thing. Somebody does the time, comes home ready to do right, and there's nowhere to land. I've been without stable housing myself. I know what that does to your head. So I stopped waiting for somebody else to fix it.
