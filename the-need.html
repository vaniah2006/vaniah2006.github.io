The Need

Georgia locks a lot of people up. Then it lets them go with almost nothing.

The numbers below aren't ours. They come from researchers and state agencies. We just live where they land.

What that looks like here

Three months goes by fast

Georgia does have some help. The Department of Community Supervision's Reentry Partnership Housing program pays for short-term housing, up to three months, for people on active state parole or probation who don't have a residence plan. That matters. We're glad it's there.

But think about three months. You're getting your ID back. Waiting on a first paycheck. Riding the bus to supervision. Trying to save up a deposit when landlords see your background and say no. Ninety days is gone before you've even caught your breath.

And if your supervision has ended, you don't qualify at all. Plenty of people walk out with no supervision and no plan.

That's why we pair the bed with structure, daily accountability, and employment readiness. The two biggest things that send people back are having no place to live and having no money coming in. We go after both at once. And veterans, who should never be in this spot, come first.

"Nobody gets out and decides to fail. They get out and run into a wall. We're trying to put a door in the wall."Chrissy Hunt

Where we work: Our primary housing is in Jones County, Georgia. We also provide additional assistance in Bibb County.

Why housing first

The stuff that actually trips people up

These aren't theories. It's the same handful of walls, over and over.

No address

No address means no job application, no bank account, often no approved parole plan. Everything starts with somewhere to sleep.

No ID

People walk out without a state ID, Social Security card, or birth certificate. Getting all three back can take weeks of running around.

No ride

Miss a supervision check-in because the bus didn't come and you're in real trouble. Transportation is a reentry issue, period.

No reference

A gap on your resume plus a background check is a hard sell. A supervisor who can say "they showed up every day" changes that.

You can't fix the whole state. You can fund one bed.
